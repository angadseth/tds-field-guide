01 Chapter 01 #

What TDS is

Tools in Data Science is a diploma-level course in the IIT Madras BS programme. Most courses teach you the theory. TDS teaches you the tools people actually use at work: the terminal, Git and GitHub, putting an app on the internet, working with AI models, scraping data from websites, and running things on the cloud.

The course content is only a reference. You learn by doing the assignments, not by watching lectures first.

It is designed by S Anand (co-founder of Gramener) and taught with instructors and TAs. Here is how the course describes itself:

  • AI will teach you. You get challenges and learn on your own, using AI and people around you. Learning by yourself is one of the skills being tested.
  • AI will evaluate you. Many answers are checked by a program or judged by an AI model. Those decisions are final.
  • It is practical. Unclear problems, messy data, tight deadlines, limited support. Just like a real job.
  • It is public. Anyone can read the content and try the assessments. Enrolled IITM students also get Discourse, project evaluation, the end term and a certificate.
"It's a hard course. Take it in your last IITM BS Diploma term. It's good for learning, maybe not for grades."tds.s-anand.net, May 2026

That line is not a joke. In the January 2025 term, 40% of the batch failed. Students still rate what they learnt highly. Go in knowing it will take more time than a normal 3-credit course.

What you will learn

The May 2026 term had a five-day bootcamp and then eight weekly modules. Don't worry about the tool names. Nobody knows these on day one. That is what the week is for.

  1. Bootcamp5 days

    Set your laptop up properly: the terminal, VS Code, running Python projects, how websites talk to each other, your browser's developer tools, and Git.

    terminal · VS Code · uv · HTTP · DevTools · curl · Git · GitHub

  2. Week 1Development tools

    The everyday toolkit: editor, shell scripts, version control, a small database, data formats, and hosting a page for free.

    VS Code · uv · Bash · Git & GitHub · SQLite · JSON/CSV · GitHub Pages · LaTeX

  3. Week 2Deployment

    Turn your code into something other people can use through a link, and run AI models on your own machine.

    FastAPI · CORS · Google login · Docker · hosting platforms · logging · Cloudflare tunnels · Ollama

  4. Week 3Working with LLMs

    Getting reliable output from AI models: good prompts, the right context, structured answers, and finding similar text using embeddings.

    prompt engineering · context engineering · structured output · embeddings · LLM CLI tools · AI coding assistants

  5. Week 4RAG

    Making AI answer from your own documents: splitting documents, searching them well, and checking whether the answers are any good.

    vector databases · chunking · hybrid search · reranking · GraphRAG · RAGAS

  6. Week 5AI agents

    AI that uses tools and takes actions, and how to keep it safe: limits, guardrails, sandboxes.

    tool calling · agent memory · multi-agent systems · MCP · sandboxing

  7. Week 6Getting data

    Collecting data from websites, PDFs, images, audio and video, ethically, and finding things with smart search.

    scraping · hidden APIs · Playwright · DuckDB · document parsing · speech & vision · Google dorking · OSINT

  8. Week 7CI/CD, security, cloud

    Automating releases, attacking and defending AI apps, and the basics of running things on cloud servers.

    GitHub Actions · Docker · LLM security · OWASP LLM Top 10 · Cloudflare · SSH · serverless · Terraform

  9. Week 8ML on the cloud

    Training, tracking, shrinking and publishing machine-learning models.

    Cloud Storage · BigQuery ML · MLflow · fine-tuning · Hugging Face · quantization · model cards

05 Chapter 05 #

GA0 and eligibility

GA0 is a self-check exam. The course site calls it the entrance exam, and it checks whether you have the pre-requisites. It opens before the term starts (13 May in the May 2026 term) and stayed open until 21 June, alongside a five-day bootcamp.

GA0 is not a gate. There is no pass mark you must clear. Skip it or score low, and you can still move ahead with the course. The course team's advice is that if you score below 40%, you should think about taking TDS in a later term. That is advice, not a rule.

Take GA0 before registration anyway. It's the quickest honest check of where you stand.

What's in it

In May 2026, GA0 had 25 short tasks covering the basics you will use all term. A few of them, to give you the flavour:

  • Move and rename files, and replace text across many files
  • Use Git, GitHub and a GitHub Action
  • Use your browser's developer tools to find something on a page
  • Write SQL, sort and filter a JSON file, calculate a variance
  • Run a small web server, and call an AI model to classify sentiment
  • "Get an LLM to say Yes", which is a prompt-writing puzzle

Skipping is allowed, but a good GA0 still helps

  1. It is one of your 9 GAs. Your GA score is the average of your best 7 out of GA0 to GA8. Skip GA0 and it simply counts as one of the two you drop.
  2. It sits among the first five GAs. To sit the end term, the average of your best 4 of the first 5 GAs must be at least 40. In May 2026 the first five were GA0 to GA4. Skip GA0 and GA1 to GA4 have to carry that average on their own.

Waiting for scores

GA0 doubts

Which email should I log in with?

Your IITM student email. Your questions are generated from your email and your marks are saved against it. Logging in with a personal Gmail means your work goes to the wrong place.

How many times can I try?

You can press Check as often as you like before the deadline. What counts is your last saved submission, so press Save after every improvement.

I got 35% (or skipped GA0). Can I still continue?

Yes. GA0 is a self-check, not a gate, and nothing stops you from moving ahead. The course team does advise students below 40% to think about a later term, so be honest about why you scored low. If you ran out of time but understood the tasks once you sat down with them, you'll be fine. If you didn't understand what the questions were asking, use the bootcamp to fix that before GA1.

GA0 looks too hard. Is the whole course like this?

GA0 covers the basics. The weekly GAs go further (deploying apps, AI agents, cloud). The difference is that you'll have a week of practice before each one. If GA0 feels impossible even with AI helping you, that tells you something.

I joined late. Will GA0's deadline be extended?

Don't plan around it. The May 2026 grading document said plainly that assignment deadlines would not be changed.

What is the bootcamp and do I have to attend?

It is a five-day bridge course: laptop setup, terminal basics, VS Code and Python projects, HTTP and browser tools, and Git. Each day has a checklist on the course site. If you already know these, skim the checklists. If not, do all five days. The whole term rests on them.

06 Chapter 06 #

How to do a GA

A GA (graded assignment) is one web page with a list of questions, each worth some marks. Every question has a Check button that tells you your score. At the bottom there is a Save button.

Check only tells you your score. Save is what records it.Your last saved submission is the one that gets evaluated.

The three kinds of questions

1. Work it out

Download a file or read some data, work something out, paste the answer: a number, a short code, a piece of JSON. If you follow the rule exactly, the marks are yours.

2. Build it and put it online

Make a small app, put it on the internet, and paste its link. The checker visits your link and tests it. Most of the deadline-night panic comes from these.

3. Write it well

A prompt, a short note, an explanation. An AI model reads it and gives marks. Be clear and follow the requested structure.

4. Something unusual

Find where a photo was taken, record a terminal session, collaborate with classmates. Read these twice. They are often simpler than they look.

A routine that works

  1. Open the GA the day it is released. Read every question before solving any. Note down the marks.
  2. Quick wins first. High marks, low effort. Leave the monsters for later.
  3. Open every hidden section. Questions often have collapsed blocks with the exact rules and formats. Missing one is the most common way to lose marks.
  4. Use AI to understand, not only to answer. Each question has an Ask AI button. Ask it to explain the task in simple words, then solve it step by step.
  5. Copy the method, not the answer. Your data comes from your email. Your friend's final answer won't match yours, but their approach will work.
  6. Check, fix, Check again, then Save. Save every time your score goes up.
  7. Test "put it online" answers like a stranger. Open your link in an incognito window before pressing Check. Free hosting sleeps when idle, so open it first to wake it up.
  8. Stuck for 30 minutes? Stop. Ask on Discourse, read the question's public GitHub discussion, or ask a friend how they approached it.
Keep whatever you deployed running until results come out. Some checks happen after the deadline.

Mistakes that cost people marks

  • Pressing Check, seeing full marks, closing the tab, and never pressing Save.
  • Skipping GA0 to GA4 because "best 7 of 9". Those decide your end-term eligibility.
  • Pasting a URL that works when you're logged in but not for a stranger.
  • "Improving" a value the question wrote down exactly. If the question gives a specific word or format, use it as written.
  • Starting two days before the deadline, when three other things are due that week.

07 Chapter 07 #

The ROE, and staying safe

The Remote Online Exam is worth 20%. It lasts 45 minutes, you take it from home, the internet is open, and it is not proctored. No system compatibility test is needed. In May 2026 it was on Sunday 2 August, 1:00 to 1:45 PM.

"The ROE is intentionally impossible."tds.s-anand.net

Nobody is expected to finish it. It tests how you choose under pressure: what to attempt, what to skip, and how fast you can get AI to do the heavy lifting.

What it looked like

The January 2026 ROE had 15 questions. Last term's (May 2026) had 12, and they were all over the place:

  • Find it: work out exactly where a Street View photo was taken.
  • Untangle data: a ledger full of look-alike characters, a route through an incident map, a simulation of how a web cache behaves.
  • Write and judge: a prompt that detects AI-written text, a short memo on where AI could help a business, insights from government telecom survey data.
  • Human ones: donate a few marks to classmates, record a short audio clip.
Next term's questions will be different. The strategy below won't be.

Strategy for the 45 minutes

  1. First 3 minutes: scan everything. Write the marks next to each question. Don't start solving yet.
  2. Quick wins first. High marks, low effort. With AI, you won't know what's easy until you try, so try each one briefly.
  3. Use AI first. Paste the question in. Spend your own brain only on what AI gets wrong.
  4. The 2-minute rule. Stuck and going nowhere? Move on and come back later.
  5. Submit the first reasonable answer, then Save. In May 2026 you could re-answer and there was no negative marking. An unsaved right answer is worth zero.
  6. Last 10 minutes: start nothing new. Save everything, including your guesses.
  7. Make friends before the exam. Some ROE questions reward working with classmates. The course faculty's own advice after the Jan 2026 ROE: work together, it helps now and later.

Staying safe: the exam's rules

  • Log in with your IITM email. Questions and marks are tied to it.
  • Internet, AI, notes and friends are allowed in the ROE. The course explicitly allows sharing code in exams, except the in-person end term.
  • Your questions are personalised. A friend's final answer won't work for you. Share methods, not values.
  • Save regularly. The last saved submission is what counts.

Staying safe: your own setup

More people lose ROE marks to a dead laptop, a logged-out account or a leaked key than to hard questions. Do this the day before.

Machine and internet

  • Laptop charged, charger plugged in.
  • Phone hotspot ready as backup internet.
  • Chrome or Edge. Turn off ad and cookie blockers for the exam site, since login uses third-party cookies.
  • Log in to the exam portal once the day before to be sure it works.
  • Empty your Downloads folder so exam files are easy to find.
  • Mute notifications. Close everything you don't need.

Accounts and keys

  • Be logged in to two AI tools, in case one hits a limit.
  • If you use API keys or tokens, check they haven't expired. Some student tokens only last about a week.
  • Never paste a key into a WhatsApp group, a shared doc or a public GitHub repo. If you leak one, cancel it and make a new one right away.
  • Don't run scripts from group chats that you haven't read. A script can quietly send your keys or login cookies to someone else.
  • Install Python packages and tools the day before, not during the exam.

Projects

Two take-home projects, 20% each, open internet. They take longer than any GA and they overlap with GAs and the ROE, so start early. The format changes every term. Here is what May 2026 looked like.

Project 1

5 – 30 Jul 2026

Five parts, mostly about using AI and the cloud to get real work done:

  1. 12.5marks

    Requirements interview. Record a short interview and share it, with your questions file, in a Google Drive folder.

  2. 12.5marks

    Differentiating model intelligence. Write one prompt that a weaker AI model gets wrong and a stronger one gets right.

  3. 12.5marks

    AI agent: cloud bucket setup. Get an AI agent to create a storage bucket on Google Cloud, and keep the agent's log.

  4. 12.5marks

    AI agent: dataset upload. Upload a given file to that storage, unchanged, and prove it.

  5. 37.5marks

    Data analyst Telegram bot. A bot that answers questions about data using an AI model. The big one.

Project 2

17 Aug – 5 Sep 2026

Eight business case studies, like a TV company's month-end numbers, a solar inverter impact claim, a customs mismatch, a spare-parts search. You play an analyst: read the data, emails and procedures, then write a short diagnostic note with:

  • a direct judgment in the first lines,
  • an evidence table (claim, source, confidence),
  • the explanations you ruled out, and why,
  • what you still don't know,
  • a safe, reversible next step.

Each note had a character limit, and the key line in the question was: "Evidence is scored before conclusion." A well-argued "insufficient evidence" could earn full marks.

What trips people up

  • Panicking over a "half score". In P1, some parts showed only part of the marks on Check because the rest is graded after the deadline. That half was often the full visible score.
  • Deleting things after submitting. Grading happens after the deadline. Keep buckets, bots, hosting and API keys alive until results.
  • Testing only while logged in. The grader is a stranger. Test your links in incognito.
  • Treating an email in the case as proof. In P2, test every claim against the data.
  • Leaving it to the last week. P1 overlapped with five GAs. P2 overlapped with GA7 and GA8.
Past terms had live evaluations on a fixed day and time. Read your term's project page the day it opens.
09 Chapter 09 #

End term and your marks

The end term (ET) is the one part of TDS that looks like a normal exam.

  • In person, at an exam centre. May 2026: Sunday 13 September.
  • No internet, no AI, no friends. The only assessment where copying isn't allowed.
  • Worth 20%, and mandatory. You only get a course grade if you attend it.
  • You must be eligible: best 4 of the first 5 GAs averaging at least 40 (see chapter 05).
Miss only the end term, with everything else done, and you get an "I" (incomplete). You can then write just the ET in a later term.

The official documents don't publish a paper pattern. According to Bharat's ET prep notes, the May 2026 end term was a theory paper: 80 marks in 90 minutes. It had 30 MCQ/MSQ questions (39 marks) and 9 written "applied AI judgment" questions (41 marks) graded by an LLM, with no negative marking. So the course is practical all term, and then the end term tests theory. Prepare for that switch.

How your final score is calculated

T = 0.2 × GA + 0.2 × ROE + 0.2 × P1 + 0.2 × P2 + 0.2 × ETMay 2026 grading document. GA = average of your best 7 of 9 GAs.

12 Chapter 12 #

Doubts, answered

The questions every new batch asks in the first two weeks.

Can I really use ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini?

Yes. The grading document says you may use LLMs and may collaborate on assignments. The course asks you to give credit to the AI and people who helped. The only exception is the in-person end term.

Can I use a solver?

Yes. In TDS everything is allowed except in the end term, and that includes solvers. Two cautions: a solver built for last term may not work once the questions change, and a wrong email gives wrong answers. Check before you Save. Links are in chapter 11.

Is copying from friends allowed? Isn't that cheating?

In TDS it's allowed, even encouraged: "Share code. Even in projects, assignments, and exams (except the final in-person end-term exam)." The reason is that real work is done in teams. In practice your questions are generated from your email, so copying the final answer doesn't work anyway. Copy the method and understand it.

Where do I submit? I can't find TDS assignments on SEEK.

That's expected. TDS uses its own exam portal. All GA, project and ROE links are on tds.s-anand.net. SEEK is not used for TDS submissions.

I pressed Check and got full marks. Am I done?

Not until you press Save. Check only evaluates. Your last saved submission is what counts.

Can I skip some GAs?

Only the best 7 of 9 count, so two can go badly. But GA0 to GA4 decide end-term eligibility, so don't skip those. If you're going to skip, skip a late one.

My friend and I did the same thing but got different answers. Who is wrong?

Probably nobody. Questions are personalised from your email, so your data and your expected answer are different from theirs.

The checker gave me 0 but my app works. Why?

Usually the problem isn't your logic, it's the setup around it: the wrong link, a server that fell asleep, something that only works while you're logged in, or an output format slightly different from what the question wrote. Open your link in incognito, re-read every hidden section of the question, and match the exact format.

The AI grader marked my answer unfairly. Can I appeal?

No. "LLM evaluations are final. No appeal." The skill being tested is writing answers that hold up no matter how the AI reads them: clear structure, the exact sections requested, no vague sentences.

Will deadlines be extended?

Plan as if they won't. Extensions have happened in past terms, but the May 2026 grading document said assignment deadlines would not be changed.

Do I have to pay for AI tools or hosting?

Most tasks can be done with free tiers. For AI, see the free AI credits in chapter 11 ($100 to $200 per provider). Some cloud tasks need an account that asks for billing or card verification, such as Google Cloud Storage in Project 1. Set these up early and watch for any charges.

When will my scores show up?

According to the course's marks dashboard, GA and ROE scores are updated one to two days after the deadline. Project scores can take a week or two, because they take longer to evaluate.

Is the ROE really impossible?

It's designed so that nobody finishes. That's fine. Scan everything, take the quick wins, use AI first, and save as you go. See chapter 07.

Is the ROE proctored? Do I need the system compatibility test?

No and no. The ROE is a non-proctored, open-internet exam, and TDS doesn't need the OPPE system test.

What happens if I miss the end term?

You need to attend the end term to get a grade. If you miss only the end term and completed everything else, you get an "I" (incomplete) and can write only the end term in a later term. Check your term's grading document for the exact options and fees.

What does the end term test?

Theory, even though the rest of the course is practical. It's in person, closed book, no internet. The official documents don't give a paper pattern, but according to Bharat's ET prep notes the May 2026 paper was 80 marks in 90 minutes: 30 MCQ/MSQ questions and 9 written questions graded by an LLM, with no negative marking. Start with TDS ET Prep.

Are there bonus marks?

Sometimes. The course may post bonus activities on Discourse, email, SEEK or the course site. They are given at the course team's discretion, so don't count on them.

Can I do TDS without being enrolled?

Yes. The course is public: anyone can read the content and try the assessments. Only enrolled students get Discourse, project evaluation, the end term and a certificate.

Who should I ask when I'm stuck?

The course's own order: ask AI first (twice). Then TAs, for learning doubts like "how do I approach this assignment?". Instructors only for exceptions, with proof, like wrong marks or an extension request. The faculty for suggestions about next term's content.

How do I keep up with announcements?

Check three places daily: SEEK notifications (the bell icon), your email including spam, and TDS Discourse.

My marks look wrong. What do I do?

Collect proof first: screenshots of your saved submission and the score, with dates. Then contact the instructors through the channel given on the course site. Remember that AI-judged scores can't be appealed.

Should I take TDS and MLP together?

MLP is listed as the co-requisite, so many people do. The bigger question is total load: TDS takes more time than a normal course, so keep that term's other courses manageable.

Nothing matches. Ask on Discourse, and maybe it ends up here next term.

Good luck. You'll be fine.
